About this experiment

A disk rolling down an incline speeds up more slowly when its mass sits farther from the axis. The travel time is the only thing you time with a stopwatch, but it carries the whole answer:

I = m r₀² (g sin θ / a − 1)

For a body rolling without slipping, a = g sin θ · m r₀² / (m r₀² + I). Measuring a = 2d / t² over the marked distance d therefore gives the total moment of inertia of whatever is rolling.

  • Time the disk down a fixed incline and turn that time into acceleration.
  • Separate the moment of inertia of the disk from that of the steel balls.
  • Compare arrangements with the same balls in different places.
Procedure
  1. Weigh the disk (m₀) and measure its radius (r₀); record h and the marked distance d.
  2. Release the disk from the start mark and stop the watch at the stop mark; repeat three times.
  3. Open the disk and put one steel ball in each outer-rim holder (Case 1); time three runs.
  4. Move all four balls to the inner rim (Case 2); time three runs.
  5. Use two inner and two outer balls in line (Case 3), then alternating (Case 4); time three runs each.
  6. Compute a = 2d / t², then I = m r₀² (g sin θ / a − 1) for every arrangement.
