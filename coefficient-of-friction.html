About this experiment

A block stays put until the pull reaches the maximum static friction, then it slides while a smaller constant force keeps it moving. Both forces are proportional to the normal force, and the constants are the two coefficients:

fs = µs n   ·   fk = µk n

On the level board n = W. On the incline the board is raised h = 15.0 cm over L = 90.0 cm, so n = W cos θ and the block's own weight pulls it W sin θ down the slope — that part of the reading is not friction.

  • Repeat on an incline, where the normal force needs cos θ.
  • Compare µk with µs for the same surfaces.
Procedure
  1. Lay the board flat. Pull the block alone with the scale, increasing the force until it just moves; record that reading.
  2. Add one brass weight at a time (up to three) and repeat.
  3. Turn the block onto its narrow face, with the same surfaces touching, and repeat all four loads.
  4. Raise one end of the board on the riser, measure h and L, and repeat on the incline.
  5. Tape paper over the contact face and repeat the static pulls on the level board.
  6. Pull the block at a steady speed on the level board, then on the incline, recording the constant force for all four loads.
