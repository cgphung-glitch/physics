About this experiment

A simple pendulum is a bob on a thread. One complete vibration — over and back — is a period T. For small swings the motion is simple harmonic, and Galileo noticed that the period depends on the length of the thread, not on the mass of the bob:

T = 2π √(L / g)

Squaring both sides gives T² = (4π²/g) L. Plot T² against L and the graph is a straight line through the origin whose slope k = 4π²/g. Solve it for g — no free-fall timing required.

  • Time 40 cycles with a stopwatch and divide to get one period.
  • Test whether the mass of the bob changes the period.
  • Test how the size of the swing changes the period.
  • Graph T² against L and find g from the slope.
Procedure
  1. Tie the thread to the support and set the length from the support to the centre of the bob.
  2. Hang the 50 g bob at L = 50 cm and pull it 10 cm aside.
  3. Start the stopwatch as you release, and stop it after 40 complete cycles.
  4. Repeat five times, then swap in the 100 g bob and repeat.
  5. Change the release distance to 5, 10, 15 and 20 cm at L = 50 cm.
  6. Change the length to 30, 40, 50, 60 and 70 cm, releasing from 10 cm each time.
  7. Plot T² against L, fit the line and read g = 4π²/slope.
