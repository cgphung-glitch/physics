About this experiment

A coiled spring stretches in proportion to the weight pulling on it, as long as the elastic limit is not exceeded. The change in length ΔL is proportional to the applied force F:

F = k ΔL

The constant k is the force constant of the spring, in N/m. A graph of F against ΔL is a straight line whose slope is k. The same spring can be measured a second way: a mass on the spring oscillates with period

T = 2π √(m / k)

so k = 4π²m / T². Comparing the two results tests whether the simple static model and the oscillation model agree.

  • Record the initial scale reading with the hanger alone.
  • Add 50 g at a time and take ten readings in all.
  • Graph weight against ΔL; find the slope and the average k with its standard deviation.
  • Time ten oscillations for a load stretched 1–2 cm; find k from the period.
  • Compare the two constants with a percent difference.
Procedure
  1. Hang the 50 g hanger so the spring is barely stretched, and record the initial scale reading.
  2. Add a 50 g slotted weight, wait for the pointer to settle, and record the new reading.
  3. Repeat until ten readings have been taken, then remove the weights.
  4. Switch to Part B, hang 100 g or 150 g so the spring stretches 1–2 cm beyond the hanger mark, and record it.
  5. Pull the mass down about 3 cm, release, and time ten complete oscillations.
  6. Divide the total time by ten to get the period, then find k = 4π²m / T².
  7. Plot F against ΔL, fit the line, and compare the slope with the mean k and with the oscillation result.

The scale reads to 0.01 cm. All loads used here stay inside the elastic limit, so the spring returns to its original length when the weights are removed.
